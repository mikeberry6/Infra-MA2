Ares Management — North American direct infrastructure fund census
Conclusion
The census identifies five current Ares Management direct-equity infrastructure vehicles that satisfy the North American inclusion test as of July 29, 2026: Ares Core Infrastructure Fund, Ares Climate Infrastructure Partners II, Ares Climate Infrastructure Partners, Ares Energy Investors Fund V and U.S. Power Fund IV. Three have an explicit North American or U.S. mandate; ACIP I and ACIP II qualify through current North American deployment attributed to their respective commercial fund complexes. (ares.com)
ACI is the only proposed repository correction. ACIP II matches the repository but remains NEEDS_REVIEW because the North American evidence identifies an ACIP II co-investment without naming the underlying asset or allocating it among the main, parallel and co-investment entities. ACI began operations on August 28, 2024 and primarily invests through direct infrastructure equity, with disclosed guidelines calling for approximately 70%–100% North American sourcing. Its commercial mechanics are perpetual-life and subscription-based, although its SEC legal form is a non-exchange-traded closed-end business development company. (ares.com)
ACIP II remained in active fundraising through year-end 2025, when Ares reported approximately $1.6 billion of equity commitments. Its fund materials describe a value-add strategy spanning development, construction, acquisition and scaling of climate infrastructure. CalSTRS separately reported an ACIP II co-investment in North America, but neither the asset nor the precise participating ACIP II legal entities were disclosed. (sec.gov)
ACIP I closed at approximately $1.4 billion in December 2021, excluding approximately $800 million of related transaction vehicles. Ares's sustainability reporting places the controlling investment in U.S.-based Apex Clean Energy in the ACIP portfolio context, and a current ACIP investment-committee member remains on the Apex board; the exact main-fund versus parallel and co-invest allocation remains unresolved. (businesswire.com)
Ares's current fund tables show that EIF V and USPF IV remain economically active despite harvesting status: EIF V retained $537 million of unrealized value as of March 31, 2026, while USPF IV retained $299 million as of December 31, 2025. Their normalized fundStatus is Financial Close, while their lifecycle descriptions preserve the current harvesting status reported by Ares. Ares and institutional materials classify the vehicles within control-oriented U.S. power and energy infrastructure strategies. (sec.gov)

ACIP I, EIF V and USPF IV have no repository matches and are proposed additions. No supplied repository row remains unreconciled, and no included commercial vehicle is double-counted through a feeder, parallel issuer, pooling entity or transaction-specific co-investment vehicle.
Exclusions and unresolved issues
The principal exclusions are infrastructure debt funds, infrastructure secondaries, the ACI-AUT access class, ACIP parallel and transaction entities, historical U.S. power funds without verified current holdings, and newly formed or ambiguously documented climate and digital vehicles that lack both fund-specific North American qualification and a complete current lifecycle record. The Ares Digital Infrastructure Global Opportunities Fund was formed in 2026, but its filing said its first sale had not yet occurred and its global label does not independently satisfy the North American test.
The principal unresolved issues are ACI's legal closed-end versus commercial open-end classification; the absence of an allowed current ACI size measure; the undisclosed asset and vehicle allocation for the ACIP II North American co-investment; the main-fund versus parallel and co-invest allocation of Apex within ACIP I; and the commercial boundaries of newer climate and digital vehicles.
Ares's predecessor and acquired-platform history was reviewed, including Energy Investors Funds, GCP International and GLP Capital Partners. The fund rows are attributed to the Ares identity used by current fund-specific evidence. No overlapping manager from the supplied manager universe required a separate attribution.
