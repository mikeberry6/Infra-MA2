Australian Super — North American direct infrastructure fund census
Conclusion
The census identifies one qualifying vehicle-level record: the AustralianSuper Infrastructure Portfolio, included as a PROGRAM_EXCEPTION rather than as a separately raised named fund. AustralianSuper’s current materials describe an ongoing direct-investment capability, identify DataBank Data Centers and a 25% interest in Transurban Chesapeake as current U.S. direct-investment highlights, and report continued FY26 performance from infrastructure holdings. The program therefore qualifies through VERIFIED_CURRENT_NA_HOLDING and is classified EVERGREEN_ACTIVE; confidence remains MEDIUM because a pension program exception cannot be high confidence and AustralianSuper does not publish a separately capitalized direct-only vehicle. (australiansuper.com)
The audited 30 June 2025 report shows A$23.684 billion of Australian infrastructure and A$24.581 billion of international infrastructure, or A$48.265 billion of level 3 infrastructure AUM in aggregate. That amount includes both internally and externally managed infrastructure; it is not represented as direct-only commitments, and no USD amount is populated because the source does not state a same-date USD equivalent. (australiansuper.com)

Exclusions and unresolved issues
The Mid-Risk portfolio is excluded because it combines Real Assets and Private Credit above the infrastructure sleeve. The North American Real Assets portfolio is a regional team/portfolio within the merged property-and-infrastructure capability, not a separately capitalized infrastructure vehicle. The Private Credit capability is debt, while Member Direct offers retail access to listed ETFs and an externally managed listed infrastructure fund. AustralianSuper’s AS Infra and AS Infrastructure trusts are controlled holding or operating entities, not independently managed commercial vehicles. (australiansuper.com)
Two supplied-manager overlaps are preserved. AustralianSuper’s audited report says it held 17.96% of Industry Super Holdings, whose subsidiary IFM Investors managed selected infrastructure and other portfolios for AustralianSuper; those externally managed vehicles remain attributed to IFM Investors. The same report shows a 23% equity holding in Generate Capital; Generate Capital’s own vehicles remain attributed to that separately supplied manager, while the stake is treated only as an AustralianSuper program holding. (australiansuper.com) The supplied manager universe confirms both manager identities are independently in scope. Pasted text
AustralianSuper was established on 1 July 2006 through the merger of the Australian Retirement Fund and Superannuation Trust of Australia; Club Plus Super merged in December 2021, and LUCRF Super members transferred effective 3 June 2022. No separate current North American infrastructure vehicle attributable to any of those predecessor or merged names was found. (australiansuper.com)
The sole unresolved conflict is capital-base semantics: A$48.265 billion is total level 3 infrastructure AUM across internal and external management, and AustralianSuper does not disclose a current direct-equity-only amount. The figure should therefore remain labeled program-level AUM, not direct-only commitments or fundraise size. (australiansuper.com)
