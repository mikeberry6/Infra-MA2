Prior notification of a concentration

(Case M.12487 – GM INFRASTRUCTURE / GIM / SAAVI / CIEM)

Candidate case for simplified procedure

(Text with EEA relevance)

(C/2026/3093)

1.   

On 28 May 2026, the Commission received notification of a proposed concentration pursuant to Article 4 of Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004 (1).

This notification concerns the following undertakings:

—

GM Infrastructure Corporation (‘GM Infrastructure’, United States), ultimately controlled by Grupo México, S.A.B. de C.V. (‘Grupo México’, Mexico),

—

Global Infrastructure Management, LLC (‘GIM’, United States), ultimately controlled by BlackRock, Inc. (‘BlackRock’, United States),

—

Saavi Energia S.à.r.l. (‘Saavi’, Luxembourg), controlled by GIM and ultimately controlled by BlackRock,

—

Controladora de Infraestructura Energética México, S.A. de C.V. (‘CIEM’, Mexico), ultimately controlled by Grupo México.

GM Infrastructure and GIM will acquire within the meaning of Article 3(1)(b) and 3(4) of the Merger Regulation joint control of Saavi and CIEM.

The concentration is accomplished by way of purchase of shares and other means.

2.   

The business activities of the undertakings concerned are the following:

—

GM Infrastructure is a US Delaware corporation formed by Grupo México for the purposes of this proposed concentration. Grupo México is a publicly listed Mexican holding company whose shares trade on the Bolsa Mexicana de Valores. Grupo México operates through three principal divisions: mining, transportation and infrastructure,

—

GIM is a global infrastructure fund manager headquartered in New York, United States, primarily focused on investing in the transportation, energy, waste, water and digital infrastructure sectors. GIM acts as the manager or adviser to a series of funds which, together with their subsidiaries, affiliates, and other entities managed or advised by GIM, operate under the trade name Global Infrastructure Partners (‘GIP’) and are therefore commonly referred to as the GIP Funds.

3.   

Saavi is active in power generation in Mexico. Its asset base comprises gas turbines, a solar photovoltaic facility, gas compression stations, and associated natural gas pipelines. Saavi primarily serves the Mexican power market in addition to specified markets in the United States.

CIEM holds power-generation assets in Mexico, including combined-cycle generation assets in Mexico and wind farms in Mexico.

4.   

On preliminary examination, the Commission finds that the notified transaction could fall within the scope of the Merger Regulation. However, the final decision on this point is reserved.

Pursuant to the Commission Notice on a simplified treatment for certain concentrations under Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004 on the control of concentrations between undertakings (2), it should be noted that this case is a candidate for treatment under the procedure set out in the Notice.

5.   

The Commission invites interested third parties to submit their possible observations on the proposed concentration to the Commission.

Observations must reach the Commission not later than 10 days following the date of this publication. The following reference should always be specified:
