Rio Tinto Infrastructure — Summary

Core Assets

  • Pilbara: ~2,000 km rail network, four iron ore terminals, ~514 MW of captive power, transmission, water and logistics infrastructure
  • Canada:
    • Saguenay: ~3.1 GW hydro, 142 km railway and Port-Alfred
    • BC Works: 896 MW Kemano hydro system, reservoir, transmission and Kitimat terminal
    • IOC: 418 km QNS&L railway and Sept-Îles export terminal
  • Simandou: >600 km multi-user railway and two ports with ~120 Mtpa capacity, consortium-owned and ultimately transferring to Guinea
  • Other: Oyu Tolgoi support infrastructure, Gladstone Power Station, Escondida desalination and port assets, Sohar power and port infrastructure, and Rio’s marine fleet

Potential Sale

  • Rio is targeting US$5–10bn of capital release from infrastructure and other assets
  • Current reporting points to a US$2–3bn transaction involving:
    • Pilbara power generation and grid assets
    • Selected Canadian infrastructure
  • Likely structure is a minority investment or structured financing, with Rio retaining operating control
  • Pilbara rail and ports have not been confirmed as part of the process
  • Dampier desalination is being pursued separately

Current Status

  • Rio said in July 2026 that approximately US$5bn of opportunities were being progressed for potential execution by year-end Rio Tinto H1 2026 results
  • Recent reporting values the infrastructure process at approximately US$2–3bn, focused on Canada and the Pilbara, with Morgan Stanley advising The Australian
  • Apollo, Stonepeak, KKR, EQT, Carlyle and Blackstone have reportedly reviewed the opportunity
  • Rio has not confirmed the final asset perimeter, structure, buyer or timing
  • The Dampier desalination sale remains non-binding and subject to diligence, financing and approvals Rio Tinto announcement

Bottom line: Rio appears to be advancing a structured minority monetisation of Pilbara power and selected Canadian infrastructure while retaining operational control
