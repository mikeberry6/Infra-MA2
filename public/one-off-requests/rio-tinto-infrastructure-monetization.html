Rio Tinto Infrastructure Monetization

Rio is pursuing minority monetizations of its Pilbara and Canadian power infrastructure, seeking to release capital while retaining majority ownership, operational control and long-term strategic flexibility

1  Identified Infrastructure Scope

Process is expected to comprise two power infrastructure opportunities

  • Pilbara Power: Existing generation, transmission and associated network infrastructure supporting Rio’s Western Australian iron ore operations
  • Canada Power: Separate Canadian power infrastructure opportunity, likely centered on the Saguenay hydro portfolio, subject to confirmation of final perimeter
  • Saguenay comprises ~3.1 GW of hydro generation across six facilities together with associated transmission and water infrastructure
  • Current scope appears focused on power infrastructure rather than Pilbara rail and port assets

2  Rationale for Monetization

Recycle capital from mature infrastructure while preserving the strategic benefits of ownership and control

  • Release capital from long-life infrastructure that can attract lower-return infrastructure capital
  • Retain majority ownership and operating control of assets critical to Rio’s mining and aluminium operations
  • Redeploy proceeds toward higher-returning mining and growth investments
  • Potentially share future sustaining and growth capex requirements with third-party capital
  • Maintain exposure to long-term infrastructure value rather than pursuing a full divestiture

3  Proposed Structure & BHP Precedent

Rio intends to replicate the BHP Pilbara Power model through a minority / synthetic infrastructure interest

  • Sell a minority economic interest of up to 49%, with Rio retaining at least 51%
  • Structure expected to resemble BHP–GIP WAIO — monetizing an economic interest in long-dated power-related cash flows rather than transferring control of the underlying operating assets
  • Provides investors with infrastructure-like contracted exposure while allowing Rio to maintain operational control
  • Rio also intends to seek a future call option, providing the ability to repurchase the investor interest over time
  • Structure creates upfront monetization while preserving strategic flexibility and potential long-term ownership consolidation

4  Process & Market Read

Process launching imminently with a targeted completion by year-end 2026

  • Going to market this week, with a desire to complete both transactions during 2026
  • Initial outreach expected to include approximately 12–15 parties
  • Buyer universe spans infrastructure funds, pension investors and insurance / credit capital
  • Early indications suggest reasonable investor interest
  • Several parties have expressed interest in pursuing both Pilbara and Canada together, rather than a single transaction
  • Whether combining the two opportunities maximizes value remains an open question — separate processes could preserve competitive tension and accommodate different investor appetites for thermal / network versus hydro exposure

Key takeaway: Rio is seeking to monetize up to 49% of two strategic power platforms using a BHP-style synthetic structure, while retaining majority control and a potential path to repurchase the minority interest over time
