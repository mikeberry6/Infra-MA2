Maintenance capex

Montauk budgets $18–20mm for non-development capital spending in FY2026. The budget includes engine overhauls and work that can increase production. The filings give no quarterly total for the capital required to sustain existing operations.

What the budget includes

Montauk names five types of non-development capex and specifically includes lifecycle work on the Bowerman engines. It gives no dollar split by category. The right-hand column explains how to assess each item for valuation. Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Capital Expenditures

What management has said

2022 · An upgrade could increase output

Montauk raised its FY2022 budget from $10–12mm to $12–14mm. The revision included Raeger work needed for stricter gas tariff requirements. Management expected the upgrade to increase daily production by about 50%. The filing gives no allocation between compliance work and added capacity. Q2 2022 Form 10-Q

2024 · The CFO called the budget maintenance capital

On the March 14 call, CFO Kevin Van Asdalan described the FY2024 range of $14–17mm as “annual maintenance capital.” He also identified $135–150mm for development. The 10-K calls the first range non-development capex and includes wellfield expansion. Both figures were forecasts. FY2023 call, closing Q&A · FY2023 Form 10-K

2025 · Routine maintenance already reduces EBITDA

The FY2024 operating expense table records $17.284mm of preventative maintenance and $8.641mm of wellfield operations and maintenance. Deducting these costs again would understate the denominator. On the Q3 2025 call, an analyst asked about maintenance capex. Management’s response concerned operating costs for wellfield and feedstock work that it could not capitalize. FY2024 Form 10-K, segment expenses · Q3 2025 call, Q&A

2026 · Overhauls and deferrals change the timing

The CFO explained that Montauk capitalizes major Bowerman engine overhauls and expenses routine manufacturer-required maintenance. In November, Montauk also identified approximately $10–15mm of reductions to discretionary and uncommitted maintenance spending, mainly beginning in 2027. The amount is a prospective reduction across planned spending. Its timing and scope preclude deducting it from FY2026 guidance to estimate recurring maintenance. FY2025 call, Q&A · Q3 2026 Form 10-Q

How the lender defines maintenance

The March 9, 2026 credit agreement defines maintenance as capital used to replace, repair or refurbish the borrower’s or its subsidiaries’ existing equipment and facilities. It separately defines expansion as capital for lender-approved work on existing equipment, collection or processing facilities expected to increase output. Both categories reduce the numerator of the fixed-charge coverage ratio. The public agreement supplies the definitions. The lender’s quarterly dollar inputs would supply the missing split. 2026 credit agreement, §1.1

Why total capex cannot supply the missing figure

In Q1 2025, three project amounts summed to $13.328mm against $11.632mm of cash capex. Subtraction gives negative $1.696mm. In Q1 2026, capital incurred was $38.644mm and cash paid was $30.867mm. Montauk attributed the difference mainly to payment timing. A project-by-project reconciliation on a common accounting basis is necessary to isolate maintenance. Q1 2025 Form 10-Q · Q1 2026 Form 10-Q

What would complete the multiple? Request quarterly maintenance and expansion amounts from the lender schedules, a reconciliation to cash paid by business segment, and the timetable for major or deferred overhauls. With those inputs, sum four quarters of comparable maintenance cash spending and deduct it once from LTM EBITDA.
