1. Geographic Expansion

  1. How does management define the geographic expansion opportunity, and which specific markets are prioritized over the next 3–5 years?
  2. What criteria does CRA use to determine whether a geography is attractive to enter or deepen?
  3. How much of the geographic growth opportunity comes from expanding with existing customers versus winning entirely new customers?
  4. What is CRA’s repeatable playbook for entering a new market?
  5. What level of customer / asset density is required for a new geography to become economically attractive?
  6. What historical markets best demonstrate CRA’s ability to execute this playbook?
  7. How does the service network enable or constrain geographic expansion?
  8. How does CRA decide between entering a market organically versus acquiring a local platform or contract book?
  9. What capital and organizational resources are required to execute the geographic expansion assumed in the forecast?
  10. How is geographic expansion reflected in the five-year forecast, and what specific assumptions underpin it?

2. Baler Market Expansion

  1. How does management define the addressable baler opportunity, and which equipment types and end markets are the primary focus?
  2. How large is the addressable baler market, what is current rental penetration, and how does this compare with the core compactor market?
  3. How much of CRA’s baler opportunity can be captured through cross-selling the existing customer base?
  4. What is CRA’s customer value proposition for renting a baler versus owning one?
  5. What is CRA’s go-to-market strategy for scaling balers, and how does it leverage the existing compactor platform?
  6. What additional operating and service capabilities are required to scale the baler business?
  7. How do baler unit economics compare with CRA’s core compactor economics?
  8. What historical evidence demonstrates CRA’s ability to successfully grow the baler business?
  9. What does the current baler pipeline look like, and how much visibility does it provide into the growth assumed in the business plan?
  10. How is baler expansion reflected in the five-year forecast, and what resources and assumptions are required to achieve it?

3. Alliance Network

  1. How does management define the Alliance Network, and what is the core value proposition to Alliance partners?

  3. What are the economics of an Alliance relationship for both CRA and the partner?
  4. How does CRA identify, recruit, onboard and activate new Alliance partners, and what has historical conversion looked like at each stage?
  5. What is the embedded growth opportunity within CRA’s existing Alliance partners?
  6. What is the remaining new-partner white-space opportunity?
  7. What historical evidence demonstrates that Alliance relationships can scale over time?
  8. What differentiates CRA’s Alliance offering and makes partner relationships durable?
  9. How does the Alliance Network support CRA’s other growth initiatives, particularly geographic expansion and baler penetration?
  10. How is Alliance Network growth reflected in the five-year forecast, and what assumptions / resources are required to achieve it?

  3. What is the embedded white-space opportunity within CRA’s existing national accounts?
  4. What is CRA’s typical “land-and-expand” playbook within a national account?
  5. What is the remaining new-logo national-account opportunity, and how does management prioritize targets?
  6. What historical evidence demonstrates CRA’s ability to win and scale national accounts?
  7. What does the current national-account pipeline look like, and what are historical win rates and sales cycles?
  8. How do national-account economics and retention compare with CRA’s broader customer base?
  9. How does the National Account Program enable CRA’s other growth initiatives?
  10. How is National Account growth reflected in the five-year forecast, and what assumptions and resources are required to achieve it?
